# The Two-Door Brand Guide

**Status:** active as of 2026-07-20. This is the copy and brand system for our two
marketing doors: **TattTester** (trust angle, tatttester.com) and **Image2Ink**
(discovery angle, image2ink.com).

## The decision this guide implements

One product. One brand. Two messages.

- **TattTester** is the primary consumer brand. It is the product people sign up
  for, the name on the app, and the name artists see.
- **Image2Ink** is two things: the name of the design-generator feature inside
  TattTester, and a marketing door at image2ink.com that leads into TattTester.
- Image2Ink is **not** a second brand. It never gets its own signup, its own
  pricing page, or its own product identity. Every Image2Ink page ends by
  handing the visitor to TattTester.

Do not relitigate this. If a page or ad needs both names, TattTester wins the
logo slot and Image2Ink is described as a feature.

## Why two doors

First-timers arrive in one of two states:

| State | What they're feeling | Which door |
|---|---|---|
| "I know roughly what I want — but will it look good on me, forever?" | Fear of regret. Permanence anxiety. | **TattTester** |
| "I want a tattoo but I have NO idea what I'd get." | Curiosity. Idea-blank. Wants to browse and play. | **Image2Ink** |

Same product answers both. The doors differ in which promise leads:
TattTester leads with *test before you ink*. Image2Ink leads with *turn any
image into ink, then meet the artist who can do it*.

## Shared foundations

These rules apply to both doors. No exceptions.

**One product, one signup.** All CTAs on both doors resolve to the same
TattTester early-access flow. Never build a separate Image2Ink account,
waitlist database, or app listing.

**Honest claims only.** Everything we say maps to a real feature: AI design
generation, AR preview on your own body, temp-tattoo export (wear the design
before you commit), artist matching, and booking. Artists refine the exported
stencil — we never sell "finished tattoo designs," and copy must never imply
the AI output goes straight onto skin. The artist is always in the loop.

**Pre-launch CTAs.** The product is pre-launch. CTAs are waitlist-compatible:
"Get early access," "Join the waitlist," "Be first in line." Never "Sign up
free," "Start designing now," or anything that promises immediate product
access.

**Banned words.** Democratize, seamless, revolutionize, unleash, empower,
game-changing, frictionless. If a sentence needs one of these, the sentence is
wrong.

**Plain English, short sentences.** Written for an 18–35 first-timer scrolling
TikTok. If your mate wouldn't say it out loud, cut it.

**Audience split.** Both doors speak consumer-first. Artists get their own
"for Artists" pitch (separate page, separate voice) — never mix artist
economics into consumer copy.

### Logo lockup rules

- **TattTester pages:** TattTester wordmark alone. Image2Ink appears only as a
  feature name in body copy ("powered by Image2Ink" is allowed in feature
  sections, never in the header).
- **Image2Ink pages:** Image2Ink wordmark in the header, but every page footer
  carries the attribution lockup: **"Image2Ink is the design engine inside
  TattTester."** The final CTA on any Image2Ink page is a TattTester CTA.
- **Ads:** the door being tested owns the creative. TattTester attribution is
  required on the Image2Ink landing page, not in the ad itself.
- Never show the two wordmarks side by side as peers. The relationship is
  always engine-inside-product.

### When to use which door

- Use **TattTester** when the message is about permanence, regret, skin,
  placement, "will I still love this," or trust in the final result.
- Use **Image2Ink** when the message is about generating, browsing, swiping,
  remixing images, or discovering what you'd even get.
- When in doubt, use TattTester. It is the default and the destination.

### Relationship to the product UI

The in-app design system (see `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`) is the punk hot-pink/black
"Tickets to My Downfall" look. That stays the product's interior. The door
palettes below are for marketing surfaces — landing pages, ads, social. The
Image2Ink door may borrow the product's hot pink as one voice in its palette;
the TattTester door deliberately does not.

## Door 1: TattTester

### Emotional register

Calm. Reassuring. The gravitas of a permanent decision, without fear-mongering.
TattTester is the steady friend who says "let's make sure" — not the parent
who says "you'll regret that." The visitor is already anxious; our job is to
lower their heart rate, not raise it.

### Voice and tone rules

- Speak to the fear directly, once, then move to the fix. Name regret; don't
  dwell in it.
- Second person, present tense. "You see it on your skin before it's
  permanent."
- Short declarative sentences. No exclamation marks in body copy.
- Concrete over clever. "Wear it for a week" beats any metaphor.
- Never joke about bad tattoos or regret. Never show tattoo-removal imagery.
- The word "forever" is allowed — it's the honest stake — but always paired
  with the thing we do about it.

### Color and typography direction

Words, not final hex:

- **Palette:** deep ink-navy as the anchor (the color of healed black ink, not
  corporate blue), warm paper and bone neutrals, and one restrained warm
  accent (skin-adjacent terracotta or amber) for CTAs. Feels like a clean
  studio, good lighting, steady hands.
- **Type:** a confident serif or humanist sans for headlines — something with
  weight and calm, not condensed shouting. Readable neutral sans for body.
  Generous line height. Sentence case, not caps-lock.
- **What to avoid:** neon, gradients, glitch effects, anything that reads
  "party." This door is the exhale.

### Imagery direction

Real skin, real light. Close crops of the AR preview on an actual arm or
shoulder. Someone looking at their forearm in a mirror. The temp tattoo at
day three, slightly lived-in. A first-timer sitting calmly in a studio chair.
People aged 18–35, diverse skin tones (the AR preview must be shown working
on darker skin, not just pale arms). No stock-photo grinning. No needles
mid-work — the moment we own is *before* the needle.

### TattTester do / don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with the fear-of-regret question, then answer it | Mock people with bad tattoos or use "tattoo fail" content |
| Say "test," "try," "wear it first," "make sure" | Say "no risk" or "guaranteed" — a tattoo is always a real decision |
| Show the AR preview and temp tattoo as the proof | Imply the AI design is what goes straight onto skin |
| Present the artist as the finisher: "your artist refines the stencil" | Suggest TattTester replaces artists |
| Use calm, spacious layouts and sentence case | Use countdown timers, urgency banners, or caps-lock hype |
| Use "Get early access" CTAs | Promise the app is live today |

## Door 2: Image2Ink

### Emotional register

Playful. Curious. Generative energy — the feeling of typing something dumb
into a generator at 1 a.m. and gasping at the result. Image2Ink is the fun
friend who says "okay but what if you DID get that." Low stakes on the surface;
the seriousness arrives later, when TattTester takes over.

### Voice and tone rules

- Open with an invitation to play, not a problem. "No idea what tattoo to
  get? Perfect."
- Casual second person. Contractions everywhere. Fragments allowed.
- Humor is welcome; sarcasm at the user's expense is not.
- Verbs of motion: turn, swipe, remix, flip, feed it, spin up.
- The artist-matching reveal is the plot twist — every Image2Ink page must
  land it: the designs lead to a real artist.
- Keep it honest under the play: it's a design and a match, not a finished
  tattoo. The artist makes it ink-ready.

### Color and typography direction

Words, not final hex:

- **Palette:** vivid gradient energy — think ink dropped in water: electric
  violet into hot pink into cyan, on a near-black base. High contrast,
  saturated, made for a phone screen in a dark room. The product's hot pink
  is welcome here as one note in the gradient.
- **Type:** bold display sans for headlines, punchy and tight — allowed to
  shout. Clean sans for body. Caps for short hype lines only.
- **What to avoid:** navy calm, paper textures, serif gravitas — that's the
  other door. Also avoid generic "AI startup" purple-blob minimalism; this
  should feel like ink, not SaaS.

### Imagery direction

Motion and abundance. A grid of wildly different generated designs from one
input image. Screen-recorded swiping. Before/after: photo in → flash-style
design out. Ink-in-water macro shots. Artist portfolio tiles appearing next
to a matched design. UGC-style phone footage for TikTok/IG, not polished
brand films. Show the range of styles (fine line, traditional, blackwork) so
"any image" reads as true.

### Image2Ink do / don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with play: "turn anything into ink" | Lead with permanence or regret — that's TattTester's job |
| Show many designs fast; abundance is the hook | Show one precious hero design like a luxury ad |
| Land the artist-match twist on every page | Let the page read as "AI art generator" with no artist |
| End every page with the TattTester attribution and CTA | Build any standalone Image2Ink signup or pricing |
| Say "your artist makes it ink-ready" | Say or imply "buy a finished tattoo design" |
| Use other people's images only as style inspiration framing | Encourage copying a specific artist's or brand's work 1:1 |

## Same fact, two voices

The proof the system works: identical facts, said through each door.

| Fact | TattTester says | Image2Ink says |
|---|---|---|
| AR preview exists | See it on your skin before it's permanent. The AR preview puts your design on your actual body, at real size. | Slap it on your arm right now — AR preview, zero needles. |
| Temp tattoos exist | Wear your design as a temporary tattoo for a week. Live with it before you decide. | Print it. Wear it. If your friends gasp, that's your sign. |
| AI generates the designs | Describe your idea and refine the design until it's exactly right. | Type literally anything — your dog, an album cover, a weird dream — and watch it turn into ink. |
| Artist matching exists | We match you with artists whose portfolio actually fits your design, so the tattoo you get looks like the one you tested. | Swipe designs. We'll find the artist who genuinely inks like this. |
| Artists refine the stencil | Your artist receives a clean stencil to refine. The final line work is always theirs. | Your design lands in a real artist's hands — they make it ink-ready. |
| It's pre-launch | Get early access and be ready before your first appointment. | Get early access. Your 1 a.m. idea deserves it. |

If a line could swap doors without anyone noticing, rewrite it.
